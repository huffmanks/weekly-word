Palm Sunday

Highlights the difference between outward proximity and inward faith, the cost of true worship and the perseverance required in seasons of tension.

When the heart is revealed

Holy Week brings moments that expose what is truly happening within the heart. These events surrounding Jesus reveal the difference between outward action and inward devotion, calling for reflection on what genuine faith looks like.

Marks of authentic devotion

  1. True worship isn’t calculated, it’s costly.
    • Genuine worship involves sacrifice, not convenience.
    • It reflects a heart that values Jesus above personal comfort or reputation.
  2. You can be close in proximity but far in heart.
    • Real connection requires intentional trust, not passive association.
    • Familiarity can sometimes lead to complacency rather than devotion.
  3. A life changed by Jesus draws attention.
    • Transformation becomes visible and noticeable to others.
    • A changed life points people toward Jesus, not self.
  4. Bold shouts don’t always translate to bold faith.
    • Genuine worship gives without concern for perception.
    • Emotional responses are not the same as enduring belief.
  5. Refuse to get stuck in the gap.
    • There is often tension between what is promised and what is seen.
    • Faith requires persistence during seasons of uncertainty.

Insight

  • The depth of worship is seen in sacrifice, not presentation.
  • Proximity to Jesus must be matched by genuine heart engagement.
  • Transformation through Jesus naturally impacts others.
  • True faith is proven over time, not in a single moment.
  • Faith must continue even when clarity is missing.
